# Flag guarded tails

GR009 flags an else-less `if` that is the last statement directly in a function or loop body and whose suite meets a size gate, so a body that nests its remaining work inside a condition is decided as a syntactic fact rather than a taste call about indentation. The candidate comes from the Tier 1 backlog of the 17-repo, 1.09 MLOC evaluation recorded in issue #54, where it was first specified as "the remainder of a callable or loop body never nests inside a trailing `if`" and measured 37% precision; adding the trailing-position requirement and the size gate — the guarded suite spans at least ten physical lines or contains a nested conditional — raised it to ~85% over the same corpus. Unlike the defensive-coding candidates in that backlog, the repair is unconditional: inverting the condition to a `return` or `continue` guard is always behavior-equivalent, since the condition still evaluates exactly once with walrus bindings included, a bare `return` is valid in generators and async generators, and a `continue` does not disturb a `for`/`else`. That is why the rule ships without a justification-comment escape hatch and with `# noqa` as the only exit.

The measurement freezes the scope. The size gate is the OR of its two arms as measured: ten physical lines, counted as the last statement's end line minus the first statement's start line plus one so interior comments and blank lines count, or a lexically contained `if` statement at any depth, including inside nested loops, `with` blocks, `try` statements, and definitions written inside the suite. A ternary expression and a `match` statement do not satisfy the second arm; both were considered and left out because neither is the shape whose nesting the measurement was about, and admitting them would widen the rule past the figure. Trailing position is direct childhood of a function body (`def` or `async def`, at any nesting depth) or a loop body (`for`, `async for`, `while`), with a leading docstring not disqualifying a body. Direct childhood is the whole mechanism and it buys two exclusions for free. The `try` skip that #54 called for falls out of it — an `if` at the tail of a `try`, `except`, `finally`, or `else` suite is not a direct child, and inverting there would either skip a `try`/`else` clause or, in a `finally`, swallow the exception in flight — as does the nested case: of two directly nested trailing ifs only the outer fires, and the inner one becomes a direct child, and a finding, on the run after the outer is inverted. A loop's `else` suite is not a loop body. Module and class bodies are outside the rule because there is nothing there to return from or continue.

The else-less requirement is where GR009 defers to Ruff. With a second branch the remainder does not nest inside a single branch, so the inversion is not a guard, and the flattening that shape wants is `RET505` through `RET508`, which unwrap an `else` after a terminating branch. Requiring an empty `orelse` keeps the two rules off the same statement instead of having them propose competing rewrites of it, and `elif` needs no separate handling since it is an `orelse` entry. The consequence is a deliberate recall hole: a long trailing `if`/`else` where only one branch is substantial is flagged by neither rule until the `else` is gone. Autofix stays out even though the transformation is mechanical, because the guard's spelling — the negation a reader will keep, and whether the inverted condition wants De Morgan's law or a helper — is the part worth a human, and because inverting the outer of a nested pair changes what the next run sees.
